Two-week sprints are a fine idea with one quiet flaw: almost nothing in the rest of life runs on a fourteen-day clock. People think in weeks. Customers think in weeks. So does your calendar.

## The weekly rhythm

- **Monday:** each person picks what they will finish by Friday. Not start — finish.
- **Wednesday:** a two-line written check-in. On track, or not, and why.
- **Friday:** show what shipped. Anything unfinished is discussed, not silently carried over.

## Why it works

A week is short enough that estimates stay honest and long enough to finish something that matters. It also makes the cost of an interruption visible: when something new arrives on Tuesday, the question becomes *what comes off the list*, and that question has an answer.

## Try it

Pick one team. Run four weeks. Keep the Friday demo no matter what. Then ask whether anyone wants the old sprint back.
